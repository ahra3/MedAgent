# GOLD 2025 Report — Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease

Source: Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD). 2025 Report.
URL: https://goldcopd.org/
License: Free executive summary available for clinical use.

## Section 1: Diagnosis and Assessment

- COPD should be considered in any patient with dyspnea, chronic cough, or sputum production, especially with a history of exposure to risk factors (primarily tobacco smoking).
- Spirometry is required to establish a diagnosis: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.70 confirms persistent airflow limitation.
- Assessment of COPD should include: severity of airflow limitation (GOLD 1-4), current symptoms (CAT score or mMRC dyspnea scale), and history of exacerbations.
- GOLD classification groups (A, B, E) are used to guide initial pharmacotherapy based on symptoms and exacerbation history.

## Section 2: Stable COPD Pharmacotherapy

- Group A (low symptoms, low exacerbation risk): a bronchodilator (short- or long-acting) is recommended.
- Group B (more symptoms, low exacerbation risk): a long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA) is recommended. If symptoms persist, LABA + LAMA combination.
- Group E (exacerbation risk, regardless of symptoms): LABA + LAMA is preferred initial therapy. If blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μL, consider LABA + LAMA + ICS (triple therapy).
- LAMA monotherapy is preferred over LABA monotherapy for preventing exacerbations.
- ICS (inhaled corticosteroids) should NOT be used as monotherapy in COPD.
- ICS-containing regimens should be considered when blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/μL and/or the patient has features suggesting asthma-COPD overlap.

## Section 3: Management of Exacerbations

- An exacerbation is an acute worsening of respiratory symptoms beyond normal day-to-day variation.
- Mild exacerbations: increase short-acting bronchodilator use.
- Moderate exacerbations: treat with short-acting bronchodilators + systemic corticosteroids (prednisone 40mg daily for 5 days) and/or antibiotics.
- Severe exacerbations (requiring hospitalization): nebulized bronchodilators, systemic corticosteroids, antibiotics if signs of bacterial infection, supplemental oxygen to target SpO2 88-92%.
- Consider non-invasive ventilation (NIV) for patients with acute respiratory acidosis.
- Following an exacerbation, review and optimize maintenance therapy to reduce future exacerbation risk.

## Section 4: Non-Pharmacological Management

- Smoking cessation is the single most effective intervention to reduce the risk of developing COPD and slow disease progression.
- Pulmonary rehabilitation is recommended for all patients with significant symptoms or exercise limitation (GOLD Group B and E).
- Annual influenza vaccination is recommended for all COPD patients.
- Pneumococcal vaccination is recommended per national guidelines.
- Long-term oxygen therapy is indicated for patients with severe chronic resting hypoxemia (PaO2 ≤55 mmHg or SpO2 ≤88%).
