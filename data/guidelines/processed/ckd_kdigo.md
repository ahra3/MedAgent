# KDIGO Clinical Practice Guidelines for CKD (Key Recommendations)

Source: Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) 2024 Clinical Practice Guidelines.
URL: https://kdigo.org/guidelines/
License: Public clinical guideline summary.

## Section 1: Definition and Classification

- CKD is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for >3 months, with implications for health.
- CKD is classified by GFR category (G1-G5) and albuminuria category (A1-A3):
  - G1: eGFR ≥90 (normal or high)
  - G2: eGFR 60-89 (mildly decreased)
  - G3a: eGFR 45-59 (mildly to moderately decreased)
  - G3b: eGFR 30-44 (moderately to severely decreased)
  - G4: eGFR 15-29 (severely decreased)
  - G5: eGFR <15 (kidney failure)
- A1: UACR <30 mg/g (normal to mildly increased)
- A2: UACR 30-300 mg/g (moderately increased)
- A3: UACR >300 mg/g (severely increased)

## Section 2: Management of CKD Progression

- Blood pressure control is essential for slowing CKD progression. Target: <120 mmHg systolic for most CKD patients (per KDIGO 2024 update, aligning with SPRINT trial data).
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs are recommended for patients with CKD and albuminuria (A2 or A3), regardless of diabetes status, to reduce proteinuria and slow progression.
- SGLT2 inhibitors are recommended for patients with CKD and eGFR ≥20 mL/min, regardless of diabetes status, to reduce kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events.
- Non-steroidal MRA (finerenone) is recommended for patients with type 2 diabetes, CKD, and albuminuria already on maximum tolerated ACE inhibitor or ARB.
- Dietary sodium restriction (<2g/day) is recommended to enhance the effect of RAAS inhibitors and reduce blood pressure.

## Section 3: Medication Adjustments in CKD

- Metformin: use with caution in CKD. Dose reduce at eGFR 30-45 mL/min. Discontinue at eGFR <30 mL/min.
- NSAIDs: avoid in CKD stages G3-G5 due to risk of acute kidney injury and further nephron loss.
- Aminoglycosides: avoid or use with therapeutic drug monitoring in CKD due to nephrotoxicity.
- Gabapentin and pregabalin: require dose adjustment in CKD based on eGFR.
- Digoxin: reduce dose in CKD as it is primarily renally cleared. Monitor levels closely.
- Contrast media: for patients with eGFR <30, weigh risks/benefits carefully. Ensure adequate hydration before and after procedures.
- Potassium-sparing diuretics and ACE inhibitors/ARBs: monitor potassium closely in CKD, especially with eGFR <45.

## Section 4: Referral to Nephrology

- Refer to nephrology when: eGFR <30 mL/min, significant proteinuria (UACR >300 mg/g), rapid decline in eGFR (>5 mL/min/1.73m² per year), CKD with refractory hypertension, persistent hyperkalemia, recurrent nephrolithiasis, or suspected hereditary kidney disease.
- Early referral is associated with better outcomes, including delayed dialysis initiation and improved transplant planning.
