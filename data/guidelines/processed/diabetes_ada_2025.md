# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes — 2025 (Key Recommendations)

Source: American Diabetes Association. Standards of Care in Diabetes — 2025.
URL: https://diabetesjournals.org/care
License: Public clinical guideline summary.

## Section 1: Glycemic Targets

- For most non-pregnant adults with diabetes, a reasonable HbA1c target is <7.0% (53 mmol/mol).
- Less stringent HbA1c goals (such as <8.0%) may be appropriate for patients with limited life expectancy, extensive comorbid conditions, a history of severe hypoglycemia, or longstanding diabetes.
- More stringent HbA1c goals (such as <6.5%) may be appropriate for selected patients if achievable without significant hypoglycemia or other adverse effects. This includes patients with short disease duration, type 2 diabetes treated with lifestyle or metformin only, long life expectancy, or no significant cardiovascular disease.
- Glycemic targets should be individualized based on the patient's characteristics, including age, comorbidities, and risk of hypoglycemia.

## Section 2: Pharmacologic Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes

- Metformin is the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for type 2 diabetes, unless contraindicated.
- Metformin should be discontinued or dose reduced when eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73m². Dose reduction should be considered at eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73m².
- For patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors with proven cardiovascular benefit are recommended regardless of HbA1c level.
- If HbA1c target is not achieved after approximately 3 months of metformin monotherapy, consider adding a second agent based on patient-specific factors including cardiovascular risk, renal function, risk of hypoglycemia, weight considerations, and cost.
- Sulfonylureas (e.g., glipizide, glimepiride) are effective and low-cost but carry a higher risk of hypoglycemia and weight gain compared to newer agents.
- Insulin therapy should be considered when HbA1c is >10%, blood glucose is >300 mg/dL, or when the patient is symptomatic with catabolic features.

## Section 3: Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease

- At least annually, assess urinary albumin (e.g., spot urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in all patients with type 2 diabetes.
- For patients with type 2 diabetes and diabetic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor is recommended when eGFR is ≥20 mL/min/1.73m² to reduce kidney disease progression and cardiovascular events.
- Optimize glucose control to reduce the risk or slow the progression of CKD.
- Metformin use requires dose adjustment based on eGFR: full dose at eGFR ≥45, reduce dose at eGFR 30–45, and discontinue at eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m².
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs are recommended for patients with diabetes and albuminuria to slow CKD progression.
- Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers may be used as alternatives in patients who cannot tolerate ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

## Section 4: Hypoglycemia Management

- Hypoglycemia is defined as a blood glucose level <70 mg/dL. Severe hypoglycemia is defined as an event requiring assistance from another person.
- Patients at risk for hypoglycemia should be asked about symptomatic and asymptomatic hypoglycemia at each visit.
- Glucose (15-20g) is the preferred treatment for conscious individuals with blood glucose <70 mg/dL, followed by rechecking in 15 minutes.
- For patients with type 2 diabetes on insulin or sulfonylureas who experience recurrent hypoglycemia, consider de-escalation of therapy.
- Glucagon should be prescribed for all patients at increased risk of clinically significant hypoglycemia.
